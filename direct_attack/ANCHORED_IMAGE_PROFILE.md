# Reachable-fiber anchoring improves the image rank-profile bound

**PROVED on paper; attribution/priority OPEN for the precise profile.**
This is a computable automaton-dependent bound, not a new bound in n alone.
Assume an SC synchronizing DFA with n>=2 and the exact image policy G.
Let E_k=max{e(S): S reachable, |S|=k}, with an empty rank contributing 0.
Define the actual reachable-fiber capacity

    kappa=max_{letter x, q} rho(x^-1{q}).

Equivalently kappa is the maximum size of a reachable image T merged by
ONE letter. It is not the maximum ordinary fiber size unless reachability
has separately justified that substitution.

## General bound and its actual word cost

There is always such an image of size >=2. Take a shortest reset word;
its last proper prefix has an image U of size >1, and its last letter
merges U. Thus 2<=kappa<=n. Pick an attaining T, letter x and target q.
The singleton {q} is reachable (SC), e({q})=1, and T is an allowed first
endpoint. Later phases from T strictly increase cardinality and pay their
FULL transport costs, visiting each rank k>=kappa at most once. Hence

    rt(A)<=P(A)<=1+G(T)<=1+sum_{k=kappa}^{n-1} E_k=:U_kappa.

In particular

    P(A)<=U_kappa<=U_2=1+sum_{k=2}^{n-1}E_k<=sum_{k=1}^{n-1}E_k.

The last comparison uses E_1>=1. The existence of a reach word for T is
not treated as free: the later G(T) chain constructs and pays for the
actual Q-to-T transport. For kappa=n there is a constant letter and the
sum is empty, giving 1. The n=1 endpoint separately gives 0.

Every singleton has e({q})<=n: transport an attaining one-step larger
image into a singleton q0, then follow an SC state path from q0 to q of
length <=n-1. This is an actual word of length <=n. The existence of a
one-step reset-target seed also agrees with the established inverse-BFS
seed convention in Section 4 of the
[Kisielewicz--Szykula primary manuscript](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1608.01268).
The one-letter merged-fiber first phase is explicitly used for completely
reachable automata in [Ferens--Szykula, Corollary 6.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.05956).
It is a known step, not a new inverse-search or anchoring idea. The profile
here retains actual reachable capacity for incomplete image families.

## A sufficient square condition and counterexample rigidity

If e(S)<=n for EVERY proper reachable image, then U_2<=
1+n(n-2)=(n-1)^2. In fact, from any singleton having e=1 every choice
of shortest extension ties costs at most this square: its first phase
costs 1, and at most n-2 remaining phases each cost <=n. This hypothesis
is false in general; it cannot be silently used as a universal theorem.

Any SC Cerny counterexample, and any failure of the global policy square,
must therefore have U_kappa>(n-1)^2. Using the proved top-rank E_(n-1)<=n,
at n>=4 this requires kappa<=n-2 and

    sum_{k=kappa}^{n-2}(E_k-n)>n(kappa-2).

Thus some INTERMEDIATE reachable rank k in [kappa,n-2] must have e(S)>n.
More quantitatively, one such rank has

    e(S)>=floor(n(n-3)/(n-kappa-1))+1.

This is a necessary condition, not a contradiction or a sufficient test.
Uniform e<=Cn only yields 1+Cn(n-kappa); for C=2 it proves the square
when kappa>=ceil((n+2)/2). No universal C=2 claim follows.

## Why the stronger rank sum still cannot prove the square

The proposal U_kappa<=(n-1)^2 is **REFUTED at state-minimal n=4**:

    a=[0,2,1,1], b=[2,1,3,0], kappa=2,
    E_1=4, E_2=6, E_3=3,
    U_old=13, U_kappa=10>9, but P=rt=8.

This is the earlier inverse-greedy obstruction, now identifying failure
of the stronger profile envelope. At n<=3 the top-rank bound and kappa>=2
give U_kappa<=the square, over ANY alphabet. The n4 scan stops at the
first failure; it does not establish a larger complete covering result.

On C_n the image rank maxima are E_k=n for 1<=k<n, and kappa=2.
The new anchored bound is EXACTLY (n-1)^2, while the old rank sum is
n(n-1). Thus the valid bound handles the classical extremal without
discarding the known one-step first phase. This alone does not control
other automata's incompatible worst images at different ranks.

Under adding transformations already generated by actual words, R stays
fixed, each old e(S) weakly decreases, and kappa cannot decrease. Therefore
U_kappa cannot increase. The n4 word-shortcut counterexample has U dropping
7 to 6 while P rises 4 to 5. This separates a valid monotone majorant from
the nonmonotone policy itself. For arbitrary alphabet additions R may grow;
no monotonicity of this profile is asserted then.

## Remaining precise charging question

For a policy chain starting with an attaining one-step image of size kappa,
each later phase S->T has cost e(S) and jump j=|T|-|S|. The jumps sum to
n-kappa. Its cost fits the square exactly when

    sum_phases [e(S)-n j]<=n(kappa-2).

This is a precise compatibility/charging target on ACTUAL images and
transport words. It is not justified by independent rank maxima. Restricting
the first endpoint to one attaining kappa may be stronger than the original
global singleton minimum target; the retained checks alone do not prove
that stronger choice always suffices. No new n-only bound or Lean result.
